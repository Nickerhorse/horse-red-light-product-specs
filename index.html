# How the horse red-light product spec audit was compiled Compiled by Nicker, a horse care brand that offers an LED light wrap for horses. No Nicker product is included in the comparison. Conflict of interest: Nicker participates commercially in the category being documented. The audit records published information for 11 products as of November 17, 2026. Its unit is a product record, including a point device alongside wearable products. Return-window comparisons use the 9 products with readable policies. The audit describes information available in reviewed sources; it does not measure device output or clinical performance. ## Which products belong in the main product count? Only records in `T1-published-specs.csv` belong in the main denominator of 11 products. `T1-supplementary-rows.csv` holds 3 additional records opened for separate contradiction checks. Adding those records would change the sample and invalidate the published main-sample counts. The products were selected for this comparison and related AI checks. The selection was not random and does not cover the entire market. Calling the sample “11 boots” would also conceal differences in device shape and intended use. Keep `product`, `stated_coverage`, and `note` beside technical values when selecting a subset. A point device and a leg wrap may publish the same kind of specification without serving the same anatomical area. ## Which sources supplied the product fields? Business product pages, policy pages, and accessible structured page data supplied the product records. The `source_type` column distinguishes official business material from retailer material. The Horseware record relies on identified retailers because the official website returned HTTP 403. Ortho Equine policy addresses returned HTTP 404; the export retains that limitation. The underlying source log records respecting access restrictions. An inaccessible policy was not reconstructed from assumptions. A retailer statement was not relabeled as an official brand statement. The `source_url` field can combine addresses and abbreviated paths, so it should be preserved as provenance text rather than treated as a single clean link. The retrieval date describes the historical reading, not a current stock or policy check. ## What do the product fields actually describe? Technical fields describe published statements with their units and qualifications. They include wavelengths, irradiance, measurement location, LED count, stated coverage, timer behavior, battery runtime, full-charge time, and ingress-protection information. Other fields preserve package and commercial context, return conditions, source identity, and notes. Read related fields together. `irradiance_mw_cm2` needs `irradiance_measurement_location`; `led_count` may distinguish LEDs from chips. A suggested session length does not establish an automatic timer. A charging-connector description does not establish a full-charge duration. Descriptions in `stated_coverage` identify what a page says about placement, not a demonstrated tissue effect. The field definitions and their interpretation limits are documented in the package's `DATA-DICTIONARY.md`. ## How does the product audit count a published value? A product contributes to a field count when the reviewed material supplies the information required by that field. Count product records, rather than mentions across several pages. Retain qualifiers such as approximate values, conflicting statements, and uncertain package units instead of turning every entry into a bare number. The source count records irradiance statements for 4 of 11 products, while numeric measurement distance is recorded for 0 of 11. Measurement-location wording is a separate category: a surface reference does not supply a numeric distance. This distinction prevents a reader from treating a published output value as a complete measurement method. None of these publication counts establishes which device performs better. ## Why does the return-window count use 9 products? Return-window counts use only the 9 products whose policies could be read. Among those policies, the compilation records 7 of 9 with a maximum window of 30 days, 1 of 9 with a 7-day window, and 1 of 9 allowing no refunds. A window exceeding 30 days appears in 0 of 9 readable policies. The unread policies remain unknown. They are neither zero-day returns nor evidence of a longer window. Policy conditions also matter: start dates, item condition, and responsibility for return shipping are retained in `returns`. A duration alone is not a complete policy comparison. ## What can a reader conclude from a missing product value? `not stated` means the information was unavailable in the reviewed material. It does not establish that a feature is absent or that every page was examined. Do not replace that text with zero. Keep regional stock qualifications and package descriptions when extracting other values.
